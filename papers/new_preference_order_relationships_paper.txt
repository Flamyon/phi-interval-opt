1. Introduction

    Many multiobjective optimization problems, the coeﬃcients (input data) associated with the objective functions involve constants
generated by measurements, estimations, statistical analyses, and son on. Consequently, these optimization problems contain uncer-
tain numerical data. If these problems are treated as a classical multiobjective optimization problem, then its optimal solutions may
not represent the phenomena initially modeled, since no method of evaluating numerical uncertainties is adopted. In order to avoid
this situation, some researchers have represented this uncertain numerical data as bounded and closed intervals of real numbers and
used tools from interval analysis (see, for example, [1,8,14,15,17,18,22,26,31]).
    It is well known that in order to introduce a concept of optimal solution for a multiobjective optimization problem in a determined
space, it is necessary to consider a concept of order relation. Thus, it is important that the order relation adopted captures the
semantic meaning of the multiobjective optimization problem, otherwise the optimal solutions may not represent the phenomena
initially modeled.
    Many studies about multiobjective interval optimization (see, for example, [14,15,22,26,31]) are based on one of the order
relations given in [16], which belong to the family of the preference interval order relations deﬁned in Section 4 of this paper. The

use of preference interval order relations is appropriated to formulate a multiobjective interval optimization problem since several of
these problems contain conﬂating objectives, that is, combination of data used to represent a given behavior of a given phenomenon.
Thus, presenting a uniﬁed approach to dealing with multiobjective interval optimization problems based on preference interval
order relationships is an interesting challenge. Overcoming this challenge is one of the main goals of this study. To achieve this goal,
a generalization of the method to generate preference ordering relationships in interval space given recently in [9] is introduced
herein. The optimization problems given by this uniﬁed approach are called 𝜑-multiobjective interval optimization problems. Some
optimality conditions for these optimization problems are presented herein.
    The other goal of this study is to present a uniﬁed approach to work with multiobjective fuzzy interval optimization problems that
are based on preference order relationships. This class of optimization problems whose elements are called 𝜑-multiobjective fuzzy
interval optimization problems, gives an expert the choice of the preference order relation based on the semantic meaning behind of a
modeled phenomenon, thus providing a reliable optimal solution for such a multiobjective fuzzy interval optimization problems. This
study also shows that a 𝜑-multiobjective fuzzy interval optimization problem is equivalent to a classical multiobjective optimization
problem. Moreover, optimality conditions for 𝜑-multiobjective fuzzy interval optimization problems are obtained as theoretical
application of the mathematical tools developed herein.
    This paper is organized as follows: Section 2 presents preliminary concepts and results in interval space and in the space of
all 𝑚-tuples of intervals where a generalized method to generate preference ordering relationships is developed. Then the order
relationships are used to introduce the notion of 𝜑-convexity for multi-interval-valued functions. Section 3 introduces the family
of 𝜑-multiobjective interval optimization problems and the concept of solution for these problems. Moreover, Section 3 shows
that some formulations of multiobjective interval optimization problems presented in the literature can be seen as a particular
optimization problem of this family and provides some optimality conditions for 𝜑-multiobjective interval optimization problems.
Section 4 presents preliminary concepts and results in the space of fuzzy intervals and in the space of the 𝑚-tuple fuzzy intervals.
This section generalizes the method to generate preference ordering relationships introduced in Section 2 and it is used to introduce
the notion of 𝜑-convexity for multi-fuzzy interval-valued functions. Section 5 introduces the formulations of 𝜑-multiobjective fuzzy
interval optimization problems as well as its solution concepts. Section 5 shows that the multiobjective fuzzy interval optimization
problems introduced in the literature, whose formulations are based on a given preference order relation, are particular cases of the
𝜑-multiobjective fuzzy interval optimization problems and it also shows the 𝜑-multiobjective fuzzy interval optimization problems
are equivalent to classical multiobjective optimization problems. Moreover, Section 5 provides some optimality conditions for this
family of optimization problems. Section 6 presents ﬁnal considerations.

5. Multiobjective fuzzy interval optimization problems

    There are many phenomena involving uncertainties that are not easy to accurately quantify. In some of these cases the use of
linguistic variables, such as illustrated in Example 4.1, helps in the study of these phenomena by means of mathematical models
based on fuzzy theory. The multiobjective fuzzy optimization problem is one important example of these mathematical models that
has applicability in several applied problems as in water resources management [4], in power plant maintenance scheduling [6], in
production planning problems [11], among others, which is one of the reasons why it has been studied extensively over the last few
decades (see, e.g., [13,19,32,24]).

6. Conclusion

    The mathematical tools presented in this study provide, through the preference order relation ≦𝜑 , the ability to generate order
relations on the space of all 𝑚-tuples of intervals by choosing appropriate automorphisms 𝜑. Using these order relations it is possible
to provide the formulations of the multiobjective interval minimization and maximization problems, (1𝑀𝐼𝑂𝑃𝜑 ) and (2𝑀𝐼𝑂𝑃𝜑 ),
that may better represent the phenomena analyzed. For example, it is well-known that the interval space does not have a structure of
linear (vectorial) space. However, there exist diﬀerent ways of providing a structure of quasilinear space to it depending on the chosen
partial order relation (see, e.g., [23]). Thus, considering a simple case, where 𝑚 = 1, if one of the objectives of a decision making is

use of the partial interval order ≦𝜑1 , where 𝜑1 is given in Example 2.2, it is not suitable since the interval space equipped with this
order does not satisfy the axioms of an ordered quasilinear interval space. To be more precise, the interval space equipped with this

order ⊆, provides a structure of quasilinear space for the interval space (see, e.g., [23]). Consequently, the use of ≦𝜑2 would be a
